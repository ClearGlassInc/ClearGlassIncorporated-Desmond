Executive finding

Bottom line: the public record supports a narrower and more defensible conclusion than the phrase “Ontario psy op” alone suggests. CJOC did issue an Information Operations annex for Operation LASER on 8 April 2020; DND says it was a mistake, suspended on 13 April and formally rescinded on 1 May, and that it did not authorize PSYOPS or military deception. At the same time, later reviews and public reporting identified information-collection and influence-related activity involving Canadians that raised authorization, risk-assessment and oversight concerns. citeturn0search0turn0search6

The Ontario dimension is material. Joint Task Force Central (JTFC), headquartered in Toronto, was the regional formation responsible for Ontario. DND publicly described Precision Information Teams as collecting publicly available geographic, demographic, COVID-19 and organizational information in areas where CAF personnel might deploy. Terminology matters

This dossier does not collapse every military information activity into “PSYOPS.” That would erase important distinctions in the evidence. The record contains at least four separate layers:

Documented

Information operations planning

An IO annex was added to Operation LASER. DND says its issuance was a mistake and that it was later suspended and rescinded.

Documented

Public-information collection

Precision Information Teams collected publicly available information relevant to potential CAF deployments. Later reporting focused on social-media collection involving Ontario.

Documented

Influence Activities training

The CAF maintained training for information-environment capabilities, including an Ontario-based capability ecosystem and the September 2020 Nova Scotia incident.

Do not overstate

“Domestic PSYOPS campaign”

That label is contested. Official sources deny authorized domestic PSYOPS or deception; the stronger claim is that domestic information activities exposed policy and control failures.

Ontario operational picture

Ontario was not simply a geographic backdrop. JTFC was headquartered in Toronto and coordinated CAF domestic support in the province. During the first wave, CAF personnel supported long-term-care facilities in the Toronto area. DND also identified JTFC Precision Information Teams as collectors of publicly available information about regions where CAF personnel might deploy. citeturn0search0turn0search23

What the public record establishes

  • CAF support to Ontario long-term-care facilities was a legitimate Operation LASER mission.
  • Precision Information Teams collected open information to help anticipate where, when and how CAF assistance might be required.
  • The controversy concerns the boundary between operational situational awareness and information activities directed toward understanding or shaping the Canadian public.
  • Public reporting later described Ontario social-media collection and sharing of information with the provincial government; the precise scope and legal basis should be treated according to the underlying records, not compressed into a single “spy” claim.

The Nova Scotia wolf exercise

The wolf incident is frequently presented as proof of a domestic PSYOPS campaign. The primary record is more specific. DND says the Halifax Rifles conducted an Influence Activities exercise at Camp Aldershot on 26–27 September 2020; the exercise tested a new loudspeaker system, used a fictional wolf scenario, and produced a mock letter on Nova Scotia Lands and Forestry letterhead. The training was not intended to be public-facing and the letter did not follow the established approval process. citeturn1search0

The incident nevertheless mattered because civilians encountered the material and provincial authorities had to publicly correct it. Independent reporting confirms that residents received the letter and that the military acknowledged responsibility for the training material. citeturn1search1turn1search2

Analytic distinction: the wolf incident is best treated as evidence of a control and authorization failure in domestic-facing training, not as proof that the CAF intentionally ran a sustained deception campaign against Nova Scotians.

Oversight, policy and the control problem

The most consequential issue was not simply whether a military unit possessed an influence capability. It was whether domestic use of that capability was bounded by authority, purpose, privacy protections, legal review, risk assessment and command oversight.

Official position

  • The IO annex was a mistake.
  • It was suspended and rescinded.
  • It did not authorize PSYOPS or military deception.
  • CAF stated it would not use those capabilities domestically without appropriate authority.

citeturn0search0

Later control requirements

The fall 2020 CDS/DM directive explicitly stated that no Information Operations activities would be executed or authorized during Operation LASER or in a domestic operation. citeturn0search6

That later directive is significant because it converts an abstract policy boundary into an operational prohibition. It is also why this dossier treats “domestic PSYOPS” as a claim requiring careful qualification rather than as a blanket description of every activity that occurred.

Implications for information resilience

1. Authority is a technical control

Information capabilities need the same explicit authorization boundaries as other high-impact operational systems. A mission label such as “support to civil authorities” cannot by itself settle whether collection or influence is permitted.

2. Collection and influence are different risk classes

Open-source situational awareness can support logistics and deployment planning. Monitoring people for sentiment or behavioural response introduces a different privacy, civil-liberties and trust profile.

3. Provenance beats narrative

For public accountability, every material claim should be traceable to a dated primary record or clearly identified independent report, with disputed propositions separated from established facts.

4. Crisis conditions expose governance debt

The pandemic compressed decision cycles. The lesson is not that information capabilities are inherently illegitimate; it is that emergency operations require pre-defined legal, ethical and command gates that remain functional under pressure.

FAQ

Did the CAF intentionally target Canadians with PSYOPS?

The official record does not support that broad formulation. DND stated that the April 2020 IO annex did not authorize PSYOPS or military deception and was suspended/rescinded. The documented controversy is about information-operation planning and collection/influence activities whose domestic authorization and controls were questioned.

Why does Ontario matter?

JTFC was the CAF regional formation for Ontario and was headquartered in Toronto. DND also publicly described Precision Information Teams operating in regions where CAF personnel might deploy, while later reporting and scholarship discuss Ontario social-media collection.

Was the wolf letter part of the COVID operation?

It occurred during the same broad 2020 period but was a separate Influence Activities training exercise in Nova Scotia. It should not be presented as an Ontario operation.

Is there evidence of an ongoing domestic CAF PSYOPS campaign in 2026?

This dossier identifies no public evidence establishing an ongoing campaign analogous to the 2020 episode. The analysis is bounded to the 2020–2021 pandemic-era record and should be updated if authoritative new records emerge.
