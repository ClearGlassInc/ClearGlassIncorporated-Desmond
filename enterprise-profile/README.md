## Company overview

ClearGlass Inc. designs and develops governed AI automation, cybersecurity and cyber-risk systems, enterprise architecture, legal-tech and regulatory intelligence workflows, OSINT and evidence-provenance systems, executive decision-support infrastructure, and business-process automation.

The organization focuses on operational problems where speed alone is not enough. High-trust systems also need traceability, explicit authority boundaries, evidence lineage, rollback planning, and human accountability. ClearGlass combines strategy, security, automation, and operations so teams can move faster without treating model output as authority by default.

The design objective is straightforward: increase execution capacity while preserving governance, provenance, accountability, and human oversight for consequential decisions.

## Mission and operating principles

**Transparency.** Map the system, expose material risk, identify dependencies, and clarify the next decision.

**Integrity.** Establish governance, audit trails, ownership, approval gates, and operating boundaries before automation scales.

**Intelligence.** Use AI agents and automation to improve decisions and reduce avoidable manual work while preserving accountability.

**Evidence.** Separate verified facts from assumptions, uncertainty, recommendations, and unresolved questions.

**Control.** Keep consequential actions inside explicit authorization boundaries and design for reversibility where practical.

## ARTEMIS / ClearGlass operating model

ARTEMIS is the ClearGlass governed operating concept for coordinating AI workflows, evidence handling, approvals, auditability, and executive decision support.

Its operating sequence is:

1. **Reveal** — expose the system state, dependencies, risks, and unknowns.
2. **Verify** — separate supported evidence from assumptions and model-generated interpretation.
3. **Stabilize** — reduce operational uncertainty before expanding scope or authority.
4. **Escalate** — route material, ambiguous, regulated, or high-risk decisions to named human owners.
5. **Execute within authorized boundaries** — allow action only when policy, ownership, evidence, and approval conditions are satisfied.

Control concepts used across the ClearGlass architecture include human approval for material or high-risk actions; evidence-linked recommendations; named ownership; policy boundaries; append-only or tamper-evident audit records where implemented; workflow versioning; model evaluation; rollback and fault isolation; safe degraded-mode behavior; and least-privilege access.

## Engineering and security posture

ClearGlass engineering work is organized around secure-by-design architecture rather than absolute security claims. Core practices include zero-trust assumptions, least privilege, secret isolation, input validation, output encoding, dependency and supply-chain awareness, deterministic testing, structured logging, provenance and evidence chains, human-gated execution, reversible changes, rollback planning, and privacy-preserving data handling.

AI outputs are treated as inputs to a governed system, not as unilateral authority. Material actions should be subject to deterministic checks, explicit ownership, approval boundaries, and auditable transition logic appropriate to the implementation.

## Enterprise operating standards

ClearGlass work can be organized into nine operating modes:

- **RECON** — inspect the environment, evidence, architecture, dependencies, and unknowns.
- **ARCHITECT** — define boundaries, interfaces, controls, ownership, and target-state design.
- **BUILD** — implement the smallest complete system that satisfies the approved architecture.
- **VERIFY** — test behavior, evidence, assumptions, failure modes, and rollback conditions.
- **DEFEND** — harden the system, reduce attack surface, monitor exposure, and improve resilience.
- **REVENUE** — connect products and workflows to measurable commercial operations without bypassing governance.
- **EXECUTIVE** — compress complexity into prioritized decisions, ownership, and action paths.
- **INCIDENT** — contain, preserve evidence, restore stable operations, and document remediation.
- **RESEARCH** — investigate emerging technology, regulation, market structure, and system design before commitment.

Work is prioritized using expected impact, confidence, urgency, effort, and risk. High-impact work with weak evidence is escalated for verification rather than presented as settled fact.
