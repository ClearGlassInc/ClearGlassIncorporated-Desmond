What "business email on your own domain" actually means

It means your address ends in your company's name — you@yourcompany.ca — instead of a free provider's. That is the visible part. The part that matters more is underneath.

The domain is the asset

A free address belongs to the provider. A domain belongs to your company, which means you can change who hosts the mail without changing every business card, invoice and directory listing you have ever issued.

Accounts survive people

When someone leaves, their mailbox is reassigned rather than lost. With personal accounts, a departure takes the client history with it — and there is no legal route to get it back.

Deliverability is configurable

You control SPF, DKIM and DMARC on your own domain. On a shared free domain you inherit whatever reputation the other users built, good or bad.

The four DNS records that decide whether your mail arrives

Most "our email goes to spam" problems are one of these four being absent or wrong. They are not optional in 2026 — major receivers now reject or quarantine unauthenticated bulk mail outright.

DNS records required for business email delivery
RecordWhat it doesWhat breaks without it
MXTells the internet which server accepts mail for your domainNothing arrives at all
SPFLists which servers are allowed to send as your domainYour mail is treated as unverified; anyone can forge your address
DKIMCryptographically signs each message so tampering is detectableReceivers cannot confirm the message is unaltered
DMARCTells receivers what to do when SPF or DKIM fails, and reports it to youFailures happen silently and you never find out

A common and expensive mistake: publishing two SPF records. The specification permits exactly one, and a second causes a permanent error that makes all your mail unauthenticated — often after someone adds a marketing tool without checking. If you take one thing from this page, take that.

Frequently asked questions

Can I keep my existing email address?

Yes, if it is on a domain you control. The address stays exactly the same — only the server behind it changes. If your current address is on a free provider's domain, the address itself has to change, because that domain is not yours to move.

Will I lose mail during the switch?

A correctly planned cutover does not lose mail. During the DNS propagation window, mail can arrive at either the old or the new server, which is why we keep the old mailbox receiving until the new one has been confirmed for at least 48 hours.

How long does DNS propagation take?

Usually under an hour for MX changes, but the published TTL on your existing records sets the floor. We lower the TTL a day before the cutover so the switch is fast when it happens.

Do I need my own domain first?

Yes. If you do not have one, registering it is the first step and typically costs CAD $15–$25 per year from a registrar. We can walk you through it, but the domain should be registered in your company's name, not ours.

What happens to email when an employee leaves?

Their account is suspended immediately, so nothing new arrives in a mailbox nobody is reading, and the contents are reassigned to whoever takes over their work. Both dates are told to you in writing.

Is my email encrypted?

In transit, yes — TLS 1.2 or better between servers that support it, which in practice is nearly all of them. At rest, stored mail is encrypted on disk. Neither of those is end-to-end encryption, and we say so plainly rather than implying otherwise.
