Free consumer email vs business email — the honest comparison

Plenty of small businesses run on free consumer mail for years without incident. This page is not an argument that they are all wrong. It is an argument about when that stops being the right call.

When free is genuinely the right answer

We would rather tell you this now than have you cancel in three months.

  • You are a sole trader with no employees and no plans for any. The reassignment and offboarding benefits do not apply to a team of one.
  • Your customers are consumers, not businesses. Nobody buying a haircut checks the domain on your confirmation email.
  • You are pre-revenue. Spend the money on the thing that produces revenue. Come back when there is some.
  • Your current setup works and nothing has forced the question. "It might be better" is not a reason to migrate a working system.

When it stops being the right answer

Usually one of these five happens, and then the decision makes itself.

  1. You hire your second person. Now there is a question of who can see what, and shared logins start looking like a plan.
  2. Someone leaves. If their address was personal, the client history goes with them and there is no route to recover it.
  3. A client or insurer asks how you handle their data. "It is in my personal inbox" is a hard sentence to write on a questionnaire.
  4. A phishing email gets paid. Domain authentication would not have stopped every version of this, but it stops the forged-from-your-own-domain version.
  5. You start bidding for larger contracts. Procurement checks. It is a small signal, but it is a cheap one to get right.

What it costs to be wrong in each direction

Switching too early

You spend roughly CAD $72 per person per year on something you did not yet need, and a few hours of setup. Recoverable, and you can cancel at the next renewal.

Switching too late

You lose a departing employee's client history, or you answer a security questionnaire badly and do not win the contract. Neither is recoverable by spending money afterwards.

That asymmetry is the entire argument, and it is why we would rather you switched a year early than a month late. It is also why we will tell you to stay put when staying put is right — a customer who cancels in ninety days costs us more than they paid.

Frequently asked questions

Is business email actually more secure than free consumer email?

Not automatically. The major free providers run excellent infrastructure. What changes is control and continuity: you own the domain, you configure the authentication, accounts survive departures, and access is per person with roles. Those are governance advantages more than raw security ones, and it is more honest to describe them that way.

Can I use my own domain with a free email provider?

Generally no — custom domains are a paid feature on essentially every major provider now. If you have an old grandfathered free plan with a custom domain, that is worth keeping until the provider ends it.

How much does business email cost in Canada?

Typically CAD $6 to $20 per person per month depending on what is bundled with it, plus roughly $15–$25 per year for the domain itself. ClearGlass Workspace starts at CAD $6.00 per person per month with annual billing, exclusive of tax.

Do I need one mailbox per employee?

One account per person, yes — that is what makes offboarding and access control work. Shared addresses like info@ or accounts@ are aliases pointing at real accounts, and they do not consume a seat.

What is the smallest team this makes sense for?

Two people is where the arguments start to bite. At one person it is mostly about how your address looks to B2B clients, which is a real reason but a weaker one.
